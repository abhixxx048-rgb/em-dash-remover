# Local Voice / Style Memory Profile

> Paste a few samples of your own writing and the tool builds a private "style fingerprint" - sentence length, contraction rate, vocabulary, punctuation habits - stored only in your browser's IndexedDB, then flags where a new draft drifts from how *you* actually write. Tier: 3. Difficulty: High. Runs: client-side (optional on-device LLM for the rewrite-bias path; never a server).

## 1. What it does

You paste two or three samples of your real writing (emails, a blog post, notes you actually wrote). The tool measures dozens of stylometric features from those samples - average and variance of sentence length, contraction rate, function-word mix, type-token ratio / lexical diversity, punctuation frequencies, em-dash and ellipsis habits, paragraph length - and saves them as a **named profile in IndexedDB on your device**. Then, when you paste a new draft (often one you cleaned on the main tool or had an AI help write), it scores how far that draft deviates from your fingerprint and highlights the specific places that "don't sound like you": sentences twice your usual length, contractions you'd normally use but the AI expanded, vocabulary you never reach for. The framing is *bias toward your own voice*, not "beat a detector" and not auto-rewrite - you keep authorship, the profile never leaves the machine, and that provable locality is the brand hook.

## 2. Why users want it (demand & search)

**Target search keywords (realistic, with demand/competition read):**
- `match my writing style` / `write in my style` - high intent, validated commercial demand, but the SERP is owned by **cloud** tools (HyperWrite "AI Write in My Style", Writeprint.ai, Grammarly's voice-learning Humanizer, Book Witch). Every one of them uploads your samples to a server. That's our exact wedge: same job, provably local.
- `writing style analyzer` / `analyze my writing style` - medium volume, mixed SERP (stylometry demos, academic tools, a few hobby pages); few polished, privacy-first consumer tools.
- `make AI sound like me` / `make ChatGPT write like me` - rising, high-intent, adjacent to the humanizer market but framed as *personalization* not evasion (safer, on-brand).
- `does this sound like me` / `is this my writing style` - long-tail, low competition, very high intent (someone staring at an AI draft asking exactly our question).
- `writing fingerprint` / `stylometry tool` / `linguistic fingerprint` - lower volume, education-led, but earns topical authority and long-tail terms (TTR, Yule's K, function words).
- `brand voice consistency checker` / `tone of voice checker` - solid B2B/marketing demand, currently dominated by paid SaaS agents (Acrolinx, Taskade, Atom Writer) that need a style guide and a login.
- `contraction checker` / `average sentence length calculator` - small but real utility searches our feature-level metrics can capture as secondary landing intent.

**Who searches and the moment they need it:** a writer who just had ChatGPT/Claude draft something and feels it's "not how I'd say it" and wants proof of *where*; a marketer maintaining one brand voice across a team without paying for Acrolinx; a ghostwriter or assistant who must match a specific person's voice; a student keeping their own voice in an AI-assisted essay; an ESL writer who has a real voice in their notes but loses it after "polishing"; and - most valuably for us - a returning user who already cleaned em dashes/quotes on the main tool and now asks the deeper question, "okay, but does this still sound like me?"

**Source domains found in research:** hyperwriteai.com (AI Write in My Style / Style Mimic), writeprint.ai (sample-library voiceprint, cloud), grammarly.com (voice-learning Humanizer), bookwitch.io (style match), revise.net and wordtune.com (tone matching), taskade.com / agent.ai / atomwriter.com (brand-voice consistency checkers), arxiv.org (stylometric feature sets; "Stylometric Detection of AI-Generated Text in Twitter Timelines" 2303.03697; optimal feature-set for style-change detection), stateofsurveillance.org and numberanalytics.com (stylometry / linguistic-fingerprint primers), researchgate.net (TTR / Herdan's C / Yule's K), rxdb.info and developer.mozilla.org and sitepoint.com (IndexedDB limits & patterns).

## 3. Target users & use cases

- **AI-assisted writers (the core)** - keep a personal profile; run every AI draft through it before sending so it reads like *them*, not the model. The "does this sound like me?" check.
- **Ghostwriters / executive assistants / agencies** - store one named profile per client/principal ("CEO voice", "Newsletter voice") and verify a drafted email or post matches before it ships, without uploading a client's private corpus anywhere.
- **Content & brand marketers** - a lightweight, free, login-free brand-voice consistency check: build a profile from approved on-brand pieces, then flag drafts that drift from the house style (sentence length, contraction policy, punctuation).
- **Students & academics** - keep their own academic voice in AI-assisted drafts; spot where a tool over-inflated vocabulary or lengthened sentences beyond how they write.
- **ESL / non-native writers** - capture their genuine voice from text they wrote unaided, then detect when a polishing tool replaced it with ornate, uniform "tool English," and pull it back.
- **Newsletter / Substack / personal-brand writers** - protect a recognizable personal style across many posts; catch the slow drift toward generic when leaning on AI.
- **Editors managing multiple writers** - compare a submission against a stored "house" or per-author profile to triage which drafts wandered off-voice.
- **Our own returning users** - after punctuation/invisible-char cleanup on the main tool, the profile is the next, stickier step that gives them a reason to come back repeatedly with the same browser.

## 4. Features - core (MVP)

1. **Build-a-profile flow** - paste 1–3 samples (the tool recommends ≥3 paragraphs / ~300+ words per the Book Witch guidance that more sample = better match), name the profile, click "Save profile." It's persisted to IndexedDB on the device, with a visible "stored only in this browser" assurance and a sample-count/word-count adequacy meter.
2. **Stylometric feature extraction** - from the samples compute, at minimum: mean and standard deviation (and coefficient of variation / burstiness) of sentence length in words; contraction rate (contractions ÷ expandable opportunities); type-token ratio and a windowed lexical-diversity measure (MATTR) so long samples aren't penalized; Yule's K richness; function-word frequency vector (the stylometric gold standard - unconscious and hard to fake); punctuation-mark frequencies per 1000 words (commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, parentheses, exclamation/question marks); em-dash, en-dash, ellipsis, and Oxford-comma habits; average paragraph length; average word length / syllables and a Flesch reading-ease baseline. Store as a versioned feature vector.
3. **Profile dashboard** - a readable summary card of the fingerprint in plain language ("You write ~16-word sentences with a lot of variation, contract heavily (78%), rarely use semicolons, never use em dashes, plain vocabulary") plus the raw metrics for the curious.
4. **Deviation scan of a new draft** - paste a draft, pick a profile, and get a single "sounds-like-you" match score (0–100) with a plain verdict, plus a normalized per-feature deviation breakdown (which features are off, and by how many standard deviations - a z-score against your own distribution).
5. **Inline highlighting of off-voice spans** - non-destructive highlights on the draft for the concrete offenders: sentences far longer/shorter than your norm, expanded contractions you'd normally use, vocabulary outside your usual range, punctuation you never use (e.g., an em dash in a profile that has none - ties straight into the main tool).
6. **Bias-toward-your-voice suggestions (non-destructive)** - for each flagged span, a concrete, click-to-apply nudge that moves *toward* the profile: "you'd usually contract this → it's" / "split this 41-word sentence" / "you don't use 'utilize' - try 'use'." Suggestions are heuristic and local; the only mutation is on explicit click.
7. **Multiple named profiles** - manage several (personal, client A, brand) with create / rename / duplicate / delete, each independently stored.
8. **Full local-data control** - export a profile as a JSON file, import it (move between your own browsers/devices manually), and a clear "Delete this profile" / "Delete all my data" that truly wipes IndexedDB. Privacy is the product, so data portability and erasure are first-class.
9. **Adequacy / honesty guards** - if samples are too short or too few, the profile is marked "low-confidence" and deviation scoring is softened with a notice ("add more of your writing for a reliable fingerprint"); never present a shaky z-score as fact, mirroring the existing engine's "no single tell is conclusive" principle.
10. **No upload, ever** - zero network calls for profiling and scanning; the page works fully offline after load, and we say so prominently.

## 5. Features - engagement & helpfulness (what makes users love it & stay)

This is the heart of the tool and the strongest expression of the brand. The profile lives in *their* browser, so every return visit is naturally personalized - the engagement loop is built in. Concrete, shippable touches:

1. **Live "sounds-like-you" match meter** - an animated, color-banded gauge (green "this is you" → amber "drifting" → red "this is the model talking") that recomputes within ~120 ms as the user edits the draft. Why: turns abstract stylometry into a number to chase; watching it climb back toward "you" as you fix sentences is the core dopamine loop that keeps people editing.
2. **Per-feature radar / bar comparison (you vs. this draft)** - a small visual overlaying the draft's feature vector on your profile's, so a single glance shows "your sentences: 16 words; this draft: 27" or "your contraction rate 78%, this draft 12%." Why: makes deviation *visible and specific* - the single most "aha" element; people instantly see which dial to turn.
3. **One-click "bias toward my voice" suggestions** - each off-voice span offers 1–3 concrete, profile-derived swaps (contract it, split it, plainer word you actually use) that insert on click into the editable copy. Why: closes the "okay, but what would *I* write?" gap that cloud rewriters answer opaquely - here you keep authorship and see exactly what changed.
4. **Plain-English profile card ("here's how you write")** - instead of a wall of numbers, a friendly one-paragraph portrait of the user's style. Why: it's genuinely delightful and shareable to see yourself described ("you write short, contraction-heavy, semicolon-averse sentences"); it makes the fingerprint feel like a mirror, not a spreadsheet, and gives a reason to build a profile at all.
5. **Personalized empty state on return** - because the profile persists, a returning user is greeted by name ("Welcome back - checking against your *Newsletter voice* profile") instead of a blank canvas. Why: instant, zero-friction personalization that no logged-out cloud tool can match, and a literal payoff of the local-storage privacy promise.
6. **"Sounds-like-you" trend over time** - log each scan's match score (locally) and show a sparkline of how on-voice the user's recent drafts have been. Why: lightweight gamification and a reason to come back; writers like watching their drafts trend more "them."
7. **Before/after voice delta** - snapshot, apply suggestions, and see the move ("Match 54 → 81 ▲; contraction rate 12% → 70%; one 41-word sentence split; 'utilize' → 'use'"). Why: makes improvement concrete and rewarding, reinforces the provable-transparency brand, and reuses the existing `diff` dependency.
8. **"Try with a demo profile" + sample drafts** - a one-click pre-baked example profile (e.g., a punchy casual voice) plus an obviously off-voice AI draft, so a first-timer sees the whole loop before risking their own writing. Why: solves the cold-start problem (you can't demo a personalization tool with no data) and proves value before any commitment.
9. **Adequacy meter while building** - a live "profile strength" bar as samples are pasted ("Good - 3 samples, 640 words; add one more for a sharper fingerprint"). Why: gentle guidance that produces better profiles, sets honest expectations, and gamifies the setup step.
10. **Per-flag "why this is off-voice" explainer** - every highlight expands to a one-line, plain reason tied to *their* data ("Your sentences average 15 words with high variation; this one is 41 - well outside your range") plus a glossary tooltip on the stylometric term. Why: education in context turns a flag into a lesson and builds trust because the tool shows its reasoning against the user's own numbers.
11. **Profile switcher with named voices** - a dropdown to flip the same draft between "My voice", "Client A", "Brand" and watch the match score and flags change. Why: power-user stickiness for ghostwriters/agencies; the multi-profile workflow is exactly what the paid SaaS tools charge for, free and local here.
12. **Export / import / share-safely** - download a profile as JSON, import on another of your devices, and (optionally) a "share my style card" image of the plain-English portrait (the *description*, not your raw samples). Why: portability respects that it's the user's data, seeds word-of-mouth via the shareable card, and the manual export is the only "sync" - no server, on brand.
13. **Keyboard shortcuts & focus flow** - `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` to scan, `[` / `]` to jump between off-voice flags, `a` to accept the focused suggestion, `Esc` to clear. Why: writers running many drafts move materially faster and stay.
14. **Cross-tool nudges** - contextual links: "This draft uses em dashes your profile never does → clean them on the main tool"; "Sentences score long → Readability Scorer"; "Want the structural AI-tells too → Human-Voice / AI-Tell Report." Why: routes traffic across the suite and makes the profile the personalized hub that the other (stateless) tools feed into.
15. **Privacy receipt / "prove it's local" panel** - a small, honest panel showing "0 network requests made," a link to view the IndexedDB record in DevTools, and a one-click "wipe everything." Why: the brand *is* provable privacy; letting skeptical users verify it (and trivially erase data) converts the privacy claim from marketing into a feature and earns trust the cloud competitors can't.
16. **Dark mode + responsive, screen-reader-announced scores** - system-theme aware, persisted, full keyboard nav, ARIA live-region announcing match-score changes, no color-only signals. Why: writers work at night and on phones; accessibility is on-brand for a clarity tool and widens reach.
17. **Empty-state guidance with a 3-step explainer** - "1) Paste a few things you wrote · 2) We learn your style (on this device only) · 3) Check any draft against it." Why: a personalization tool is confusing cold; the three-step framing plus the demo button lowers bounce at the most fragile moment.

## 6. UX / UI notes

- **Layout:** Two top-level modes sharing one page. **Build mode** - a multi-sample paste area (add/remove sample slots), a live profile-strength meter, a name field, and a "Save profile" action; on save it flips to the plain-English profile card + metrics. **Check mode** - left/top: the read-only-but-copy-back-editable draft as the highlight surface; right/bottom (stacks on mobile): the match gauge + verdict, the you-vs-draft feature comparison, the grouped off-voice findings punch-list, and the active-profile switcher. A persistent, unobtrusive "Profiles" manager (list, switch, export, delete) lives in a side drawer.
- **Input/output model:** the profile is the durable state (IndexedDB); the draft is transient and the report is fully derived and live (no mandatory "Analyze" button, but offer one for explicit re-run / accessibility). The only mutations are explicit: accepting a voice suggestion, or "copy edited text back."
- **States:**
  - *First run / no profile:* the 3-step explainer, "Try a demo profile," and the build form.
  - *Returning with a profile:* personalized greeting, default to Check mode against the last-used profile, draft area focused.
  - *Building (low signal):* profile marked "low-confidence," with a friendly "add more of your writing."
  - *Processing large draft:* subtle shimmer on the rail; never block typing.
  - *On-voice result:* celebrate it ("This sounds like you - nice") so the tool is trusted not to over-flag.
- **Microcopy tone:** warm, personal, non-judgmental, ownership-framed - "here's where it stops sounding like you," "keep your voice," never "wrong" and never "beat the detector." Always pair a flag with a constructive, profile-derived next step.
- **Mobile:** modes become tabs; the rail collapses to an accordion under the draft; the feature comparison scrolls horizontally; suggestion popovers anchor as a bottom sheet; tap targets ≥44px.
- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA):** no color-only signals - every highlight and gauge band carries an icon/label and an accessible name; the findings list is fully usable without the visual highlights; match-score changes announced via `aria-live="polite"`; full keyboard operation; contrast-checked bands; respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 7. Technical implementation (client-side)

**This is the heaviest of the suite, but stays in-browser.** It has three parts: a feature extractor, a comparison/scoring layer, and a persistence layer.

**Feature extraction (vanilla JS, linear).** Tokenize with `Intl.Segmenter` (built into modern browsers, zero bundle) for words/sentences with a regex fallback (`/(?<=[.!?])\s+/` hardened with an abbreviation guard for "U.S.", "e.g.", "Dr."). Use `\p{L}` with the `u` flag so accented/ESL text counts. Compute per profile:
- **Sentence length:** mean, SD, coefficient of variation (reuse the existing `burstiness()` CV helper in `src/lib/ai-tells.ts`).
- **Contraction rate:** count contractions (`'`-form regex over a known contraction list) over expansion opportunities (e.g., "do not"/"it is" patterns) → ratio. Must handle straight vs. curly apostrophes (U+0027 / U+2019).
- **Lexical diversity:** type-token ratio plus **MATTR** (moving-average TTR over a fixed window, ~50 words) so long samples aren't unfairly flattened, and **Yule's K** for richness (frequency-spectrum formula, stable across length).
- **Function-word vector:** frequency of a fixed ~150–400 function-word list (articles, prepositions, pronouns, conjunctions, auxiliaries) - the stylometric workhorse because it's unconscious and topic-independent. Stored as a normalized vector.
- **Punctuation profile:** counts per 1000 words for each mark, plus explicit em-dash (U+2014), en-dash (U+2013), ellipsis (U+2026 and "..."), Oxford-comma, semicolon, and parenthesis habits - reuse the code points already used by the cleaner.
- **Structure:** mean paragraph length, mean word length / syllable estimate, Flesch reading-ease baseline (reuse the Readability Scorer's syllable counter).

Persist as a **versioned feature vector** (schema version + extractor version) so future metric changes can re-derive or invalidate old profiles cleanly.

**Comparison / scoring.** Store, per feature, the sample mean and SD. For a new draft, compute the same features and a **z-score per feature** (how many of the user's own SDs away the draft is). Combine into a weighted distance - Euclidean/Mahalanobis-style over normalized features, or cosine distance for the function-word and punctuation vectors - and map to a 0–100 "sounds-like-you" score via a calibrated, monotonic transform. Inline highlighting needs **character offsets**: run sentence-length and contraction/vocabulary checks with offset capture (same offset-aware matcher the Human-Voice report and Passive-Voice highlighter need), so the work is shared across the suite. Surface the top-deviating features, not all of them.

**Persistence (IndexedDB).** Wrap raw IndexedDB with **`idb-keyval`** (~600B) or **`idb`** (a few KB) for promise-based, readable call sites - raw IndexedDB lacks promises and is verbose (MDN, SitePoint). One object store `profiles` keyed by id, value = `{ id, name, createdAt, schemaVersion, features, sampleStats, lastMatchScores[] }`. Profiles are tiny (KB), so the multi-MB+ quotas (disk-bound; Firefox prompts only above ~50MB blobs per RxDB/MDN) are irrelevant - but handle quota-exceeded and private-mode failures gracefully with a localStorage fallback and a clear message. Provide true deletion (`clear()` / `deleteDatabase`) for the privacy promise, and note IndexedDB persists indefinitely unless the user clears site data or the browser's eviction kicks in - so offer export for durability.

**Performance.** Extraction and scoring are linear scans; typical samples/drafts (<10k words) are sub-frame. Debounce live scoring ~120 ms; for very large drafts (>~20k words) run extraction in a **Web Worker** (no network, so it stays on-brand and keeps the UI responsive). The function-word vector is the only memory-notable structure and is still trivial.

**Optional rewrite-bias path (the only place an LLM is even considered).** The WANTLIST framed this as "bias rewrites." We deliberately keep v1 **non-destructive and heuristic** (suggestions, not generation). If we ever add true voice-matched rewriting, it must use an **on-device model** - Chrome's built-in Prompt API / WebLLM / WebGPU - fed the local profile as the system prompt, with **zero server calls**, opt-in, and the privacy promise intact. A server LLM would break the entire premise and must never be the path. This is exactly the differentiator versus HyperWrite/Writeprint/Grammarly, which POST your samples to their cloud.

**Edge cases & failure modes:** too little sample → low-confidence profile, softened scoring; non-English → gate or note the function-word list is English-tuned (extraction degrades gracefully); code blocks / quoted text in a draft → "ignore quoted blocks" toggle so quoting an AI sample doesn't tank the score; lists/tables → exclude from sentence-length stats (a bulleted list is legitimately uniform); private/incognito mode → IndexedDB may be ephemeral, warn the user; mixed-author samples (user pasted someone else's text) → can't detect, but the adequacy/honesty copy frames the profile as "only as good as the samples."

## 8. Competitors & how we differentiate

- **HyperWrite (AI Write in My Style / Style Mimic / Imitate Writing Style)** - analyzes a reference and rewrites to match. Weaknesses: cloud-only (your samples and drafts are uploaded), subscription, and it *generates* rather than teaching you where you drifted. **Our wedge:** on-device fingerprint, non-destructive guidance, you keep authorship.
- **Writeprint.ai** - builds a "voiceprint" from an uploaded sample library and humanizes AI content to sound like you (uses GPT-4/Claude/Gemini server-side). Weaknesses: uploads a corpus of your private writing to a third party, paid, opaque. **Our wedge:** the corpus never leaves your browser; we show the features and the math.
- **Grammarly Humanizer (voice-learning) / Wordtune / Revise AI** - adapt to your voice over time, cloud, account-gated, embedded in a paid suite. **Our wedge:** free, no login, local, single-purpose, transparent.
- **Brand-voice consistency SaaS (Acrolinx, Atom Writer, Taskade / Agent.ai brand-voice checkers)** - strong for teams but require a configured style guide, a login, and server processing. **Our wedge:** a free, instant, local approximation - build a profile from a few on-brand pieces and check drafts with no setup or upload.
- **Book Witch style match** - paste a style sample and rewrite to it; cloud, rewrite-first, no persistent personal profile. **Our wedge:** a *saved, reusable* profile and a deviation report, not a one-shot rewrite.

**Consolidated positioning:** the only **provably private, on-device** voice/style profiler - it learns how *you* write, keeps that fingerprint on your machine, and tells you exactly where a draft stops sounding like you, framed as keeping your voice (not bypassing a detector) and wired into a suite of cleaners rather than a paid silo.

## 10. Build effort & priority

- **Effort:** High - the most ambitious tool in the suite. Net-new work: the stylometric feature extractor (contraction detection, MATTR, Yule's K, function-word vector, punctuation profile), the z-score/distance scoring layer, the IndexedDB persistence + profile management UI (CRUD, export/import, true-delete), and the build/check two-mode UX. The optional on-device rewrite-bias path is a separate, later bet.
- **Dependencies on existing code:** reuses `burstiness()` and word/punctuation primitives from `src/lib/ai-tells.ts`, the cleaner's Unicode code points (U+2014/U+2013/U+2026/U+2019), the Readability Scorer's syllable/Flesch counter, the `diff` dependency for before/after, and the shared offset-aware matcher (built once for the Human-Voice and Passive-Voice tools - build it there first, reuse here). Adds one small dependency: `idb-keyval` or `idb`.
- **Sequencing:** This is a **Tier-3 strategic bet - build it last**, after the Tier-1 evergreen pages establish the tool hub and internal-link graph and the Tier-2 Human-Voice / AI-Tell Report ships the offset-aware matcher and the quality-not-bypass positioning. By then we have the metrics primitives (burstiness, syllables, function-word lists) and a returning audience whose browser already trusts us - the perfect substrate for a persistent, personalized profile. Ship v1 **non-destructive and heuristic** (profile + deviation report + suggestions); treat any on-device generative rewrite as a fast-follow once the local-LLM (WebGPU/Prompt API) story is mature, never compromising the zero-upload promise that is the whole point.
